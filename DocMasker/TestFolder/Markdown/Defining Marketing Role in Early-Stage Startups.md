**User:**

How can we define a marketing role in a early stage startup?

**Kimi:**

In an early-stage startup, the marketing role is a hybrid of strategy, research, and hands-on execution. It is less about “running campaigns” and more about discovering whether the market actually needs what you are building and, if so, how to reach that market efficiently. Below is a practical framework you can adopt.

1. Core Mission: Achieve product–market message fit
The first marketer’s north-star metric is to validate the problem, the audience, and the wording that makes people care. That means the role is closer to “Head of Experimentation” than “Head of Marketing Communications” .
2. Pre-launch responsibilities (0–6 months)
• Customer &amp; market research – 10–15 customer interviews every two weeks, competitor tear-downs, and channel mapping (where do prospects hang out, how do they currently solve the problem) .
• Positioning &amp; messaging – craft one-sentence value propositions for each early segment; build the first pitch decks and landing pages to test resonance .
• Brand basics – choose a name, tone, and visual identity that will not embarrass you later, but do not over-polish; the brand will evolve .
• Launch plan – define wait-list vs. beta vs. public launch tactics, timelines, and success metrics .
3. Post-launch responsibilities (6–18 months)
• Growth experiments – run small paid-ads tests, SEO content sprints, referral loops, and partnership campaigns; double-down on whichever channel yields the lowest CAC .
• Instrumentation – set up the first KPI dashboard (traffic, trial-to-paid, CAC, LTV) and review it weekly .
• Sales enablement – create the first battle cards, objection-handling sheets, and case studies that the founder can use in early deals .
• Iterative messaging – refine value props with every cohort of new users; archive what does not convert .
4. Team structure options
• Solo marketer (often a founder) – handles everything from interviews to Google Ads; outsource design and dev tasks .
• First hire – a “full-stack” growth marketer who can research, write copy, and launch ads; fractional CMO for strategic oversight if budget allows .
• 2–10 employees – add one marketing generalist and a part-time content or paid-media freelancer .
5. Skills that matter most
• Structured interviewing &amp; note-taking
• Rapid landing-page or no-code tooling
• Basic analytics (Google Analytics, Mixpanel)
• Copywriting that converts
• Comfort with ambiguity and weekly pivots

A concise job description you can post today
Title: Founding Growth Marketer
Mission: Find the message and channel that turn strangers into paying customers within 90 days.
Key tasks: customer interviews, positioning docs, launch experiments, KPI dashboard.
Must-haves: 2+ growth experiments shipped, fluent with GA4 or Mixpanel, excellent written English.
Nice-to-haves: early-stage startup experience, design eye, SQL.

By defining the role this way, you keep the focus on learning and traction rather than on polished-but-untested marketing activities.
